All fifteen keygen pairs favour EXP-005. Kyber512's median reduction is 5.18%; Kyber768's is only 0.48%; Kyber1024's is 1.36%. The Kyber768 effect is similar in size to movements in operations with unchanged arithmetic, so I do not claim that its small speed change isolates the benefit of removed Barrett calls. It needs confirmation in the final repeat-session evaluation.

Kyber512 and Kyber768 encap/decap show small positive movements despite executing no keygen error-vector path. Kyber1024 encap is mixed, with two positive and three negative pairs and a -0.12% median; decap is slower in four of five pairs with a -0.50% median. These remain part of the outcome. I do not relabel regressions as proven noise or claim all operations improved. Across all operations there are 38 positive and seven negative pairs.

Keygen favours EXP-005 in both order groups. The groups contain three and two pairs, so this does not establish order independence. The Kyber1024 encap group changes sign by order, consistent with retaining it as a small mixed effect rather than a robust benefit. The stronger block-3 movements across several Kyber768/1024 operations are retained without assigning an established cause.

I compared enc_derand, dec, indcpa_enc and indcpa_dec in the actual timing binaries. Each parameter's normalised instruction bodies match between variants. The two KEM entry addresses are unchanged, but the IND-CPA encryption/decryption functions move by 16 bytes for Kyber512 and 32 bytes for Kyber768/1024. This directly demonstrates layout changes. It does not quantify their effect or prove that every reachable address/instruction is identical. Layout, compiler effects and environmental variation remain possible contributors to the observed control-operation movements.

The [predeclared protocol](../error_ntt_timing.md) uses five adjacent pairs per parameter, 100 warm-ups, 1000 measured exchanges per process, seed 1 and guest logical CPU 0. EXP-003 runs first in odd blocks. Both variants are fresh normal -O3 builds with the same GCC 13.3.0 executable and flags. The recorded guest is x86_64 Ubuntu 24.04.2/WSL2 on a reported i9-12900KS with 24 visible logical CPUs; host/power/background/physical-core facts remain unknown. Guest affinity does not prove physical-core placement.

Timer diagnostics report CLOCK_MONOTONIC_RAW nanoseconds, 1 ns resolution and 11–12 ns minimum empty-pair probes. Resolution is not accuracy. All raw observations are retained, spanning 15803–1190035 ns; no overhead subtraction or outlier deletion occurred. Fixture generation, verification and output are outside measured intervals; deterministic APIs exclude operating-system randomness. Repeated fixtures and inner durations are not independent experimental units. Five matched pairs in one session do not establish formal significance, cross-machine reproducibility or a universal speed guarantee.

## Security and mechanism

Actual timing source identities and all 24 reviewed keygen/NTT/reducer bodies match the accepted [RUN-0001 validation](EXP-005-RUN-0001.md). The conditional bounds remain 23299/26627 for Kyber512 and 23298/26626 for Kyber768/1024, before the retained final public-key reduction. skpv and all earlier inverse-input contracts remain intact. Correctness/invalid-input transcripts, counts and exercised UBSan results are inherited from that verified validation, not newly rerun or inferred from speed.

The confirmed mechanism removes K*256 keygen Barrett calls while retaining Montgomery counts. The generated keygen bodies also grow by 17/33/40 bytes and local stack changes by +16/+16/0 bytes. Keygen canaries, endbr64 and page probes remain; the earlier EXP-002 dot-product canary tradeoff persists. Wrapper bypass, register/spill and layout effects occur alongside the removed reductions, so this run does not measure an isolated Barrett-call cost.

Public control of the changed error-vector path and unchanged reducer/NTT assembly supports a scoped implementation review. The Kyber1024 keygen body retains public-matrix rejection sampling. The performance harness does not classify secret inputs and is not a leakage test. Whole-program constant-time behaviour, statistical leakage resistance, power/EM and fault behaviour, portability, exhaustive memory safety and final ML-KEM conformance remain unestablished or unassessed.

The user clarified in ACT-0014 that the supervisor expects an evaluation of how optimisations affect implementation security, with VTune suggested optionally. I therefore make the [focused security assessment](../implementation_security.md) a required remaining practical stage. The current assembly/bounds/UBSan evidence contributes to it but does not replace executed secret-flow and timing-leakage checks.
