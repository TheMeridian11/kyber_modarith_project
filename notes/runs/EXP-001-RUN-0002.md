## Measured effects

Improvement is 100 * (1 - candidate process median / baseline process median), calculated within each matched pair. The estimate below is the median of five paired improvements. Duration columns are separate medians of process medians, so their ratio need not equal the primary paired estimate. Ranges are observed minima/maxima, not confidence intervals.

| Parameter set | Operation | Baseline median (microseconds) | EXP-001 median (microseconds) | Median improvement | Pair range | Faster pairs |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Kyber512 | keygen | 18.7270 | 17.9550 | 4.12% | 3.87% to 5.32% | 5/5 |
| Kyber512 | encap | 21.3375 | 20.0555 | 5.87% | 5.66% to 7.15% | 5/5 |
| Kyber512 | decap | 27.8405 | 26.1080 | 6.01% | 5.91% to 7.34% | 5/5 |
| Kyber768 | keygen | 32.8310 | 32.3120 | 1.68% | 0.53% to 2.69% | 5/5 |
| Kyber768 | encap | 35.1745 | 34.8675 | 0.87% | -0.47% to 1.34% | 4/5 |
| Kyber768 | decap | 43.4275 | 43.0505 | 0.86% | -0.35% to 1.24% | 4/5 |
| Kyber1024 | keygen | 49.8835 | 46.1980 | 7.43% | 6.69% to 8.23% | 5/5 |
| Kyber1024 | encap | 55.2840 | 50.6125 | 8.55% | 8.05% to 9.36% | 5/5 |
| Kyber1024 | decap | 65.0405 | 59.6510 | 8.43% | 8.14% to 9.16% | 5/5 |

Kyber512 improved by approximately 4.12%, 5.87% and 6.01% for keygen, encapsulation and decapsulation. Kyber1024 improved by 7.43%, 8.55% and 8.43%. Every pair for these parameter sets favoured the candidate, including both execution orders. This supports a benefit under the measured conditions.

Kyber768 keygen was faster in all five pairs, with a smaller 1.68% median improvement. Encapsulation and decapsulation each had a median below 1% and one slower pair, both in block 5. I classify those two effects as inconclusive at this measurement budget rather than assert a reliable sub-one-percent gain.

Medians grouped by first variant are positive for every parameter/operation, so the apparent benefits do not disappear simply by reversing order. Their magnitudes differ and there are only three baseline-first and two candidate-first pairs. This is a descriptive order check, not proof that order, thermal state or host scheduling has no effect. The small Kyber768 results remain uncertain.

## Interpretation and limitations

The reduction counts and assembly establish the intended mechanism. Timing establishes a benefit for the larger observed effects in this run. The different gains across parameter sets also demonstrate why fewer reducer calls cannot be converted directly into a runtime percentage. These results do not isolate why Kyber768 benefited less; compiler-generated surrounding code, layout and execution conditions are possible influences, not measured explanations. I do not add profiling work merely to speculate about a cause.

There are five matched pairs per parameter set in one WSL run. The 1000 fixture indices are repeated across blocks. Individual rows and process repetitions may share conditions; I do not treat the 90000 rows as independent experiments or claim formal statistical significance. Host power mode, background activity and physical-core placement remain unknown. The timings cover deterministic APIs and valid exchanges; they exclude operating-system randomness and do not measure invalid-ciphertext performance.

All outliers and both negative paired effects remain in the raw data and analysis. No extra repetition is required merely to make every result positive. A later claim of a small Kyber768 improvement would need further planned complete pairs or controlled confirmation. General claims across machines or sessions require evidence beyond this run.

The accepted correctness/range/UBSan and scoped assembly findings remain applicable to the tested source. A speedup adds no proof of whole-program constant-time behaviour, statistical leakage resistance, power/EM security or fault resistance.

## Decision and subsequent study

I retain EXP-001 as a validated research reference, with performance support for Kyber512/Kyber1024, a small consistent Kyber768 keygen improvement and inconclusive Kyber768 encapsulation/decapsulation effects. I close the initial EXP-001 evaluation with those limits and proceed to [EXP-002 vector-wide fusion](../experiments/EXP-002-wide-dot.md).

The subsequent [EXP-002](../experiments/EXP-002-wide-dot.md) extended fusion across vector components, with its own bounds, validation and assembly review. Its timing study used freshly built EXP-001 controls. This run's evidence remains unchanged.
