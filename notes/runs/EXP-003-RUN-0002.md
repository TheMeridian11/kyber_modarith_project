Encapsulation and decapsulation consistently favour EXP-003 across all parameters and both execution orders. Their smallest individual effect is Kyber768 decapsulation block 4, +3.684%; none crosses zero. Kyber512 keygen crosses zero and does not establish an improvement; Kyber768 is slower in all five pairs, while Kyber1024 is faster in all five. I preserve these small observed gains/regressions without claiming they are universal or dismissing them as proven noise.

Keygen calls no inverse transform and has unchanged reduction counts. As a focused follow-up, I compared keypair_derand and indcpa_keypair_derand in both timing binaries for each parameter. Their normalised instruction sequences and entry locations are unchanged. This does not inspect every reachable function or explain the small timing shifts. Code placement elsewhere, shared environment and measurement variability remain possible contributors; no causal allocation has been established. I do not credit keygen movement to removed inverse reductions.

The order groups are small and unequal; their differences do not establish absence of drift or ordering effects. Kyber768 candidate block 4 has higher medians for all three operations and smaller encap/decap benefits. I retain it and every other observation. No outlier removal, timer-overhead subtraction or outcome-driven rerun was performed.

I used the [specified protocol](../invntt_timing.md): five adjacent pairs per parameter, 100 warm-up and 1000 measured exchanges per process, seed 1, parameter order 512/768/1024, EXP-002 first in odd blocks and EXP-003 first in even blocks. Timing uses CLOCK_MONOTONIC_RAW nanoseconds on uninstrumented normal builds. All 30 timer reports show 1 ns clock resolution and minimum empty-pair probes of 11–12 ns; these are diagnostics, not a claim of 1 ns measurement accuracy. Fixture generation, shared-secret checks and output formatting remain outside timed intervals, and deterministic APIs exclude operating-system randomness.

GCC 13.3.0 and its executable SHA-256 match the accepted review, using the recorded -O3/-fomit-frame-pointer/noexecstack flags. Ubuntu 24.04.2 under WSL2 reports x86_64, i9-12900KS, 24 logical CPUs and kernel 5.15.167.4-microsoft-standard-WSL2; Python is 3.12.3. Affinity is guest logical CPU 0. Host OS details, power mode, background load and physical-core placement remain unknown. Guest affinity does not prove placement on a particular physical core.

The 90000 raw durations are nested in 30 processes and reuse fixtures; the comparison unit is a matched process pair, not each inner duration as an independent experiment. This single session does not establish confidence intervals, formal significance, reproducibility on other machines or a universal ranking. I do not add or multiply separately measured median effects to claim total gain over the original reference; that needs a later direct comparison.

## Verification and security interpretation

All 54 steps exited zero. I verified 108 raw-stream hashes, six executable hashes, fifteen support hashes, source identities and committed bytes, the 35 restored EXP-002 files, tool hashes, dataset order and the linked validation/review identities. Every raw row passes the strict CSV/roundtrip checks. Independent median/min/max calculations agree with the parser, and the versioned analysis reproduces all 90 process summaries, 45 pairs and nine aggregate rows byte for byte. All eighteen saved inverse/Montgomery/Barrett function bodies match the ACT-0011 review and fresh disassembly of the retained binaries.

The [correctness and security review](EXP-003-RUN-0001.md) remains applicable: centred inputs bounded by 1664, inverse sums/differences bounded by 26624, valid product/reducer ranges, unchanged Montgomery scale and tested byte compatibility including invalid-input paths. Source hashes confirm those assumptions were not changed between validation and timing. No new full validation or leakage test was needed or performed for this evidence review.

Counts explain the intended mechanism: 896 -> 128 Barrett calls per inverse transform, with 1152 Montgomery calls retained. However, the inverse body also grows from 347 to 458 bytes, local stack use falls from 104 to 72 bytes including saved registers (excluding return address/callees), and compiler loop/layout choices change. This experiment measures their combined effect, not the isolated time cost of a Barrett call.

No new coefficient-dependent branches or addresses were identified in the reviewed inverse/reducer bodies; both reducers are unchanged. Neither inverse version has a local canary, so EXP-003 removes none there. EXP-002's earlier dot-product local-canary tradeoff remains inherited. Passing timings and sampled checks do not establish whole-program constant-time behaviour, memory safety, statistical or power/EM leakage resistance, fault resistance, portability, production approval or final ML-KEM conformance.

## Decision and next experiment

I close EXP-003's initial evaluation as supported for its targeted encap/decap benefit and keep it as a research reference with the documented keygen tradeoffs. I preserve all raw data and historical manifests unchanged.

The next proposed treatment is [EXP-005: defer error-vector reduction in key generation](../experiments/EXP-005-error-ntt.md), conditional on retained EXP-003. It targets only the sampled error vector e, which is added once before an existing final public-key reduction. Other forward reductions and the centred inputs required by EXP-003 stay intact. EXP-004 remains deferred for the previously reserved interaction/combination question. New bounds, manual application and correctness/count/assembly validation precede any timing claim.
