They cover public/secret keys, valid ciphertexts and shared secrets, corrupt/random ciphertext secrets, malformed-secret-key output, and noncanonical-public-key ciphertext/secret output. ## Range, representation and consumer argument

I rechecked the [experiment's bounds](../experiments/EXP-005-error-ntt.md). CBD gives error coefficients within ETA1=3/2/2 for every possible noise seed. Seven forward stages obey the conservative induction B -> B+3328, with |zeta| <= 1664. The largest product is 33231744, within int32_t and the Montgomery input interval [-109084672,109084671]. Every butterfly result stored in int16_t is representable. Final raw error bounds are 23299/23298/23298.

After unchanged poly_tomont, pkpv is bounded by 3328. The addition therefore stays within 26627/26626/26626 before the retained final polyvec_reduce. That reduction produces the same unique centred residues for public-key serialization. Error e is not a multiplication input, inverse input or serialized secret-key vector. skpv retains its reduction. No Montgomery operation or scale conversion is removed, and all reductions establishing EXP-003's centred inverse inputs remain intact.

The proof is conditional on this call path, current parameters/noise range and unchanged consumers. It does not justify removing poly_ntt's reduction globally or feeding raw e into the wide dot product. Passing UBSan alone does not establish narrowing safety. Existing signed conversions and negative shifts remain tied to the recorded compiler/target assumptions.

## Generated code, memory and security findings

I inspected all saved keygen/forward-NTT/reducer bodies in the six normal validation binaries and extracted the same four functions from the three hash-verified EXP-003 timing binaries. Fresh disassembly agrees with saved candidate assembly; symbol-table sizes independently agree with parsed instruction coverage. The EXP-003 keygen bodies also match the saved original-reference bodies after relocation normalisation. The record retains instruction offsets, operands, symbolic call/branch targets and function sizes.

In each candidate keygen body the compiler keeps one polyvec_ntt call for skpv and unrolls the new public K-loop into two, three or four direct ntt calls at fixed error-polynomial offsets. The final polyvec_add, polyvec_reduce and serialization calls remain. No error-coefficient-dependent branch or address is introduced by this replacement.

The forward NTT (254 bytes), Montgomery reducer (22 bytes) and Barrett reducer (36 bytes) have identical normalised instructions across original reference, EXP-003 and EXP-005 for each parameter; parameter namespaces aside, they also match across parameter sets. NTT branches and its conditional move use public loop state; table/array indexing is public. Reducers use fixed arithmetic. I found no division or indirect control transfer in these reviewed bodies.

I distinguish this changed path from the whole keygen function. Kyber512's entry body has the canary-failure branch; Kyber768 also has its public matrix-vector loop. Kyber1024 inlines the existing matrix rejection sampler, whose acceptance branches depend on values derived from the public matrix seed. It also contains fixed noise/matrix loops and the canary check. This is not a claim that the whole keygen body is branchless, that its duration is independent of public inputs, or that every callee has received a new security audit.

The subsequent timing run measures removed reductions, wrapper bypass, register/spill changes and code/stack layout together. It cannot isolate the duration of individual Barrett calls. Larger stored representatives also change internal bit patterns: public control flow alone does not assess power/EM leakage or faults. Whole-program constant-time security, statistical leakage resistance, portability to other targets, exhaustive memory safety, final ML-KEM conformance and production approval remain unestablished. The optimisation does not alter cryptographic parameters or noise distributions.
